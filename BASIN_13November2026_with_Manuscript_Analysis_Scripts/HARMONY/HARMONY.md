# HARMONY: BASIN Normalization Pipeline
## BASIN Pipeline 2 — Methods Description and User Guide

---

## Overview

HARMONY is the normalization pipeline at the heart of BASIN. It takes aligned
BAM files and a set of BASIN anchor regions (BARs) as input, computes BASIN
scale factors from a fixed canonical coverage representation, and produces
canonical BASIN-scaled bigWigs for quantitative QC and downstream analysis.
HARMONY also generates canonical CPM bigWigs for CODA (Pipeline 3), and can
optionally generate separate centered and/or extended derivative bigWigs for
visualization or specialized downstream analyses.

HARMONY is implemented in Python and is invoked via a single command with a
configuration file. It is designed to run on HPC clusters via SLURM, though it
can also be run interactively. A SLURM submission script (`run_harmony.sh`) is
provided in the `slurm/` directory.

All steps are logged to both stdout and a persistent run log file. Every run
produces a self-contained output directory with a full manifest recording the
config, sample provenance, tool versions, and environment — making runs
reproducible and auditable.

## Conceptual Motivation

Traditional normalization methods assume that total signal is roughly constant
between samples. This assumption often fails in CUT&RUN and CUT&TAG experiments
where histone marks can shift globally, mutant conditions alter chromatin
occupancy genome-wide, or spike-in normalization is unavailable or unreliable.

The difference between CPM and BASIN can be stated simply:

**CPM asks:** "What fraction of the library maps here?"

**BASIN asks:** "How strong is the signal relative to empirical background?"

By anchoring normalization to IgG-derived background regions rather than total
read count, BASIN preserves true genome-wide gains or losses in chromatin signal
that CPM would suppress. The key biological insight is that stronger antibody
enrichment reduces nonspecific IgG binding — so IgG signal is inversely related
to true signal strength, and can be used as a proxy for the normalization
denominator without adding any exogenous material.

### When BASIN Is Most Useful

BASIN is applicable to any CUT&RUN or CUT&TAG experiment where IgG nonspecific
enrichment is measurable across the genome. It is not limited to broad chromatin
marks — transcription factors, histone modifications with punctate enrichment,
and other chromatin-binding proteins are all appropriate targets.

BASIN is particularly valuable when the experiment involves conditions where
global signal levels may differ between samples — for example, when profiling a
histone modification in a mutant that affects the total abundance of that mark,
or when comparing conditions where chromatin occupancy is expected to change
genome-wide. In these cases, CPM normalization suppresses the very differences
that are biologically meaningful, while BASIN reveals them.

BASIN is also useful as a general replacement for spike-in normalization, since
it requires no exogenous material and avoids the pipetting error, input
equivalence assumptions, and organism-specific complications that spike-in
approaches introduce.

### Assumptions and Caveats

BASIN requires that IgG nonspecific enrichment is measurable at BAR regions —
meaning that sequencing coverage over those regions must be sufficient to
compute a reliable median. Experiments with very low total read counts or very
low cell inputs may not have adequate coverage, and CODA will flag samples where
anchor support is insufficient.

BASIN normalization has been validated as robust across a wide range of BAR
files, including the three universal Drosophila files provided and experiment-
specific files generated by users. BASIN normalization is robust across BAR 
files derived through different approaches. Both universally-derived files 
(built from diverse tissues and laboratories) and experiment-specific files 
(built from IgG controls within a single experimental system) produce reliable 
normalization when appropriate parameter choices are made during BAR discovery.
The key properties of a good BAR file are captured by three metrics: sufficient 
panel size (typically 300–2000 regions), low replicate CV reflecting technical 
reproducibility, and minimal genotype shift reflecting absence of normalization
bias across conditions. These thresholds were empirically calibrated using six 
Drosophila CUT&TAG datasets profiling diverse chromatin features (H3K36me2, 
H3K36me3, H3K27ac, pan-H4ac, RNAPII-S2P, and RNAPII-S5P). BAR files varying 
substantially in region count, region length, and SEACR stringency all produced 
comparable normalization performance on this validation set, supporting the 
conclusion that a range of reasonable parameterizations will yield reliable 
results.The universal Drosophila files provided with BASIN were selected based 
on these criteria and are appropriate starting points for most Drosophila 
experiments. The universal Drosophila BAR files distributed with BASIN were 
additionally validated across a broad range of independent datasets, including 
CUT&RUN experiments profiling H3K27me3, pan-H4ac, and H3K9me3 in Wing Disc, and 
CUT&TAG experiments in S2 cells, confirming consistent normalization 
performance across assay types, antibody targets, and experimental contexts.
See Pipeline 1 (OVERTURE) for guidance on generating BARfiles for other 
organisms or experimental contexts.

BASIN's core assumption is that IgG nonspecific enrichment is not systematically
different between the conditions being compared. Experiments where the
biological manipulation itself alters nonspecific binding may violate this
assumption. An additional implicit assumption is that BAR regions are present in
stable copy number across samples — copy number variation at anchor loci could
introduce systematic bias. When assessing quantitative changes, conditions being
compared MUST not have large copy number differences between loci. Some copy 
number differences can be absorbed by the trimmed median.

## Canonical Coverage Contract

BASIN separates the **normalization/QC representation** from optional downstream
representations. BAR definition in OVERTURE, BAR quantification in HARMONY, and
baseline QC in CODA must use the same spatial coverage geometry.

The canonical BASIN representation is fixed to:

- **1-bp bins**
- **uncentered reads** (`--centerReads` is not used)
- **unextended reads** (`--extendReads` is not used)
- the run's configured minimum MAPQ

The first three settings are not user-configurable for canonical outputs. MAPQ
remains configurable because filtering reads by mapping quality does not change
BAR coordinates; the chosen threshold is applied consistently to canonical
NONE, CPM, and scaled tracks within a run and recorded in
`run_manifest/coverage_contract.tsv`.

Centering is available only for **separate downstream derivative tracks**. A
centered, unextended derivative changes where the fixed read footprint is
represented but never becomes an input to BAR scaling or CODA. Read extension
is also restricted to derivative tracks and is explicitly labeled because
extending coverage across inferred fragment length makes integrated coverage
fragment-length-dependent; such tracks should not be used for direct
cross-library quantitation unless fragment-length distributions have been
validated as comparable.

## Quick Start

For Drosophila CUT&RUN or CUT&TAG experiments, start from the `HARMONY/`
directory (the Slurm launcher expects to find
`harmony.py` there):

```bash
mkdir -p logs
cp templates/config.yaml config.yaml
cp templates/samples.tsv samples.tsv

# Edit samples.tsv: replace example rows with your data and absolute BAM paths.
# Edit config.yaml: set run_id_prefix and change universal_bed to:
#   templates/anchor_files/dm6/IgG_consensus_fdr0p005_relaxed_90pct_filter_away_lt50bp.chr.bed
# The copied config's samples_tsv: samples.tsv setting is already correct.

sbatch slurm/run_harmony.sh config.yaml
```

Relative paths for `samples_tsv`, `universal_bed`, and `run_root` are resolved
relative to the location of `config.yaml`. The original
`templates/config.yaml` instead uses `anchor_files/dm6/...chr.bed`, which is
correct **when the template is used in place**. The example manifest contains
placeholder BAM paths; example BAMs are not distributed.

HARMONY will handle duplicate marking, baseline bigWig generation, BAR signal
summarization, scale factor computation, and scaled bigWig generation
automatically.

### Choosing a BAR File

Three universal Drosophila BAR files are provided in the 
`templates/anchor_files/dm6/` directory. All three were derived from 28 IgG 
CUT&RUN and CUT&TAG datasets spanningmultiple independent laboratories, tissues, 
cell types, and both assay types.

- **`...AUTOSOMES_ONLY.bed`**: BARs on
  autosomes only, with histone gene peaks removed. Use this file when the sex
  ratio of your samples is unknown or mixed, or when you want to avoid any
  potential sex-chromosome copy number effects on normalization.

- **`...NO_HISTONE_GENE_PEAKS.bed`**: BARs on all chromosomes including sex
  chromosomes, with histone gene peaks removed. Use this file when your samples
  have a known, consistent sex ratio and you want to include sex-chromosomal
  anchor regions.

- **`...chr.bed`** (**recommended default for most experiments**): The full
  BAR file including all chromosomes and histone gene regions. Use this file
  for standard experiments not involving histone copy number mutations.

If you are working in another organism, see Pipeline 1 (OVERTURE) for generating
organism-specific BAR files. BASIN normalization is robust across BAR files —
results are consistent as long as coverage over anchor regions is adequate.

## Input Requirements

**BAM files.** One per sample, aligned to your reference genome and sorted by
coordinate. BAM files do not need to be deduplicated before running — HARMONY
handles duplicate marking and removal internally (see Duplicate Handling). BAM
index files (`.bai`) are required; HARMONY will generate them automatically if
they are missing.

**BAR file (`universal_bed`).** The BED file of BASIN anchor regions used for
normalization. BARs are sometimes referred to as UIP regions (Universal IgG
Peaks) in earlier versions of this documentation. For Drosophila, three
universal BAR files are provided in the `templates/anchor_files/dm6/` directory 
— see Choosing aBAR File in the Quick Start section for guidance on which to 
use. All three werederived from 28 Drosophila IgG CUT&RUN and CUT&TAG datasets 
spanning multiple independent laboratories, tissues, cell types, and both assay 
types. See Pipeline 1 (OVERTURE) for generating organism-specific BAR files.

**Sample manifest (`samples_tsv`).** A tab-separated file listing all samples to
be processed. Required columns are:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `sample_id` | Unique sample identifier. Used in output filenames and QC summaries. No spaces; underscores recommended. |
| `bam_path` | Absolute path to the aligned BAM file. |
| `antibody` | Antibody used (e.g., H3K27me3, H3K36me3). |
| `genotype` | Genotype or condition label (e.g., wildtype, mutant). |
| `sex` | Sex of the sample (e.g., Male, Female, Mixed, NA). |
| `replicate` | Replicate number or identifier. |

Comment lines beginning with `#` are ignored. An example manifest is provided in
`templates/samples.tsv`.

**Configuration file (`config.yaml`).** A YAML file specifying all run
parameters. A fully annotated template is provided in `templates/config.yaml`
and all parameters are described in the Configuration Reference section below.

## Run Directory Structure

Each HARMONY run produces a timestamped output directory under `run_root`
(default: `outputs/runs/`). The directory name is constructed from the
`run_id_prefix`, a timestamp, the username, the hostname, and the SLURM job ID,
ensuring that every run is uniquely identified and traceable.

Within the run directory, HARMONY creates the following subdirectories:

```
run_dir/
├── run_manifest/
│   ├── config.resolved.yaml      # Config with all paths resolved to absolute
│   ├── samples.resolved.tsv      # Sample manifest as HARMONY read it
│   ├── sample_provenance.tsv     # Per-sample BAM/bigWig provenance
│   ├── coverage_contract.tsv     # Canonical BASIN geometry/MAPQ/trim contract
│   ├── versions.txt              # Tool versions
│   ├── environment.txt           # Run environment
│   └── commands.sh               # Commands executed
├── bams_prepared/
├── bigwigs_baseline/             # Canonical NONE + CPM tracks
├── signal_summary/
├── scales/
│   ├── scales_long.tsv
│   └── retained_bars.tsv         # Exact retained BARs per sample/drop
├── bigwigs_scaled/               # Canonical BASIN-scaled tracks
└── bigwigs_downstream/           # Optional derivative tracks; never read by CODA
```

The `run_manifest/` directory is designed to make runs fully reproducible. The
`config.resolved.yaml` records all parameters with absolute paths, so the exact
conditions of any run can be reconstructed. The `sample_provenance.tsv` records
for each sample which BAM was used, how it was prepared, and where the baseline
bigWig came from. The `commands.sh` records every shell command executed, in the
order it was run.

## Pipeline Steps

HARMONY executes the following steps in order.

### Step 1: BAM Preparation

Before generating any bigWig files, HARMONY prepares a BAM file for each sample
appropriate to the configured duplicate handling mode. This step is lazy — BAMs
are only prepared when needed, and existing prepared BAMs from the current or a
prior run are reused when available, avoiding redundant computation.

See Duplicate Handling for a full description of the preparation logic.

### Step 2: Canonical Baseline BigWig Generation

For each sample, HARMONY generates two **canonical** bigWigs:

- `{sample_id}.NONE.bw`: raw coverage used as the BASIN scale basis.
- `{sample_id}.CPM.bw`: depth-normalized coverage used by CODA for baseline QC
  and drop-stability diagnostics.

Both use 1-bp bins, no centering, and no extension. They also use the same
configured `min_mapq` and prepared BAM/duplicate policy. CPM is an analytical QC
input, not a freely configurable display layer.

If `reuse_baseline_from_run` is set, HARMONY validates the prior run's
`coverage_contract.tsv`. Pre-contract runs require the explicit
`allow_unverified_legacy_baseline_reuse: true` override after manual review.

### Step 3: BAR Signal Summarization

HARMONY runs `multiBigwigSummary` over the BAR intervals:

- `universal_peaks.NONE.rawCounts.tsv`: canonical raw BAR coverage used for
  scale-factor computation.
- `universal_peaks.rawCounts.tsv`: canonical CPM BAR coverage used by CODA.

Each BAR value is mean per-bp coverage across the interval. Because CPM is a
positive scalar transformation of raw coverage, zero/nonzero BAR structure is
unchanged between the two canonical summaries.

### Step 4: Scale Factor Computation

For each sample and drop fraction, HARMONY:

1. removes BAR rows that are zero across **all samples**;
2. ranks the remaining BAR values from highest to lowest;
3. removes `round(N × drop_fraction)` BARs from the top;
4. computes the median of the retained values. This value is reported in
   `scales/scales_long.tsv` as `median_kept` and is referred to throughout the
   BASIN documentation as `medBAR`;
5. sets `scale_factor = 1 / medBAR`.

Python's `round()` behavior is the authoritative BASIN trim rule. HARMONY writes
`scales/scales_long.tsv` and also `scales/retained_bars.tsv`, which records the
exact BAR coordinates retained for each sample/drop so CODA does not need to
reconstruct tie or boundary decisions.

#### Using `medBAR` for downstream differential analysis

`scales/scales_long.tsv` reports both the retained BAR median (`median_kept`,
i.e. `medBAR`) and the corresponding HARMONY coverage multiplier
(`scale_factor = 1 / medBAR`). These quantities have opposite directions and are
intended for different downstream uses. The `scale_factor` column is used to
generate BASIN-scaled coverage tracks, whereas `medBAR` is the corresponding
denominator-style normalization quantity for count-based differential analysis.

For frameworks such as DESeq2, raw peak or window counts should be retained and
BASIN normalization supplied through the package's sample-level size-factor or
offset interface rather than by pre-scaling the count matrix. In DESeq2,
`medBAR` values can be used as user-defined size factors; rescaling them to a
geometric mean of 1 is optional but convenient for consistency with DESeq2
conventions. Other frameworks, including edgeR and DiffBind, also support
externally supplied sample-level normalization information through
package-specific normalization-factor or offset interfaces. Users should
therefore supply the BASIN scaling relationship in the convention required by
the downstream package rather than using the HARMONY coverage multiplier
directly.

**IMPORTANT: DO NOT SUBSTITUTE `scale_factor` FOR `medBAR` IN SOFTWARE THAT
EXPECTS DENOMINATOR-STYLE SIZE FACTORS; `scale_factor` IS THE RECIPROCAL
QUANTITY USED FOR COVERAGE-TRACK MULTIPLICATION.**

### Step 5: Canonical BASIN BigWig Generation

HARMONY applies each valid scale factor directly to the same canonical coverage
representation using `bamCoverage --normalizeUsing None --scaleFactor <sf>`.
Canonical outputs remain 1-bp, uncentered, and unextended and are written under
`bigwigs_scaled/drop{fraction}/`. CODA reads only these canonical scaled tracks.

#### Step 6: Optional Downstream Derivative BigWigs

HARMONY can additionally generate derivative CPM and/or BASIN tracks under
`bigwigs_downstream/`. These never feed scaling or CODA.

- `downstream_center_reads: false` together with `downstream_extend_reads: false`
  produces aligned read-footprint coverage with no centering or fragment extension. 
  This preserves the original read geometry: coverage remains at the mapped 
  read positions and therefore reflects the cut-site-associated representation 
  rather than inferred fragment midpoints.

- `downstream_center_reads: true` produces read-length footprints centered on
  the inferred paired-end fragment. deepTools requires `--extendReads` internally
  for `--centerReads` to use paired-end fragment geometry, so HARMONY passes both
  CLI flags for this mode. The resulting coverage remains read-length rather
  than fragment-spanning. These tracks retain the original normalization
  factors but relocate coverage from the aligned read positions to the inferred
  fragment midpoints, providing a proxy for epitope position rather than
  displaying the original cut-site-associated read coverage.

- `downstream_extend_reads: true` with centering disabled produces genuinely
  fragment-spanning derivative coverage. Filenames include
  `extended_fragmentweighted`, and HARMONY prints a warning. IMPORTANT:
  Although the original normalization factors are applied, integrated coverage
  depends on fragment length. Differences in fragment-length distributions
  between samples can therefore compromise quantitative comparisons.

- `downstream_center_reads: true` together with `downstream_extend_reads: true`
  is rejected. deepTools does not expose these as independent final-output
  states: its internal `--extendReads` is already required to give
  `--centerReads` paired-end fragment geometry. Rejecting the combination
  prevents a fragment-spanning output from being promised when deepTools
  actually returns centered read-length footprints.

- `downstream_bin_size` controls derivative resolution only.

A centered, unextended BASIN derivative is named like
`sample.scaled.drop0.20.dedup.centered.unextended.bw`.

`make_fragment_splits` remains a fragment-size **filter** on canonical scaled
tracks and does not turn on read extension.

## Duplicate Handling

HARMONY's duplicate handling is one of its most carefully designed components,
because the treatment of PCR duplicates substantially affects signal-to-noise in
CUT&RUN and CUT&TAG data. HARMONY supports two modes, configured via
`dedup_mode`.

### dedup_mode: remove (default)

In remove mode, HARMONY uses deduplicated BAMs for both baseline bigWig
generation and scaled bigWig generation. The deduplication workflow is:

1. If a deduplicated BAM already exists from the current or a prior run, it is
   reused.
2. Otherwise, HARMONY checks whether the input BAM already has duplicate flags
   (samtools view -f 1024). If it does, HARMONY proceeds directly to
   deduplication by filtering flag 1024 reads.
3. If the input BAM has no duplicate flags and `mark_duplicates_if_missing` is
   true, HARMONY runs samtools markdup (via sort-by-name → fixmate → sort-by-
   coordinate → markdup) to add duplicate flags, then filters them.
4. The prepared BAM and its mode of preparation are recorded in
   `sample_provenance.tsv`.

### dedup_mode: keep

In keep mode, HARMONY retains all reads including duplicates. If the input BAM
has duplicate flags already, it is used as-is. If it does not and
`mark_duplicates_if_missing` is true, HARMONY runs samtools markdup to add flags
(which does not remove reads — it only marks them), and uses the resulting BAM.

The appropriateness of deduplication depends on two factors: library complexity
and the type of enrichment being profiled. For high-complexity libraries
profiling broad chromatin domains, deduplication has minimal effect on signal
because true signal reads are diverse and unlikely to appear as duplicates. For
low-complexity libraries or very low cell inputs, deduplication can remove a
substantial fraction of genuine signal reads, making keep mode preferable. For
experiments profiling sharp, punctate peaks, duplicate removal is generally more
important because PCR duplicates are harder to distinguish from real signal.
HARMONY can be run in both modes on the same samples to compare results — use
`reuse_baseline_from_run` to avoid regenerating baseline bigWigs.

### Reuse Logic

Prepared BAMs are cached in `bams_prepared/` within the run directory. HARMONY
checks for existing prepared BAMs in the following order:

1. Current run `bams_prepared/` directory
2. Prior run `bams_prepared/` directory (if `reuse_baseline_from_run` is set)
3. Build a new prepared BAM

This reuse logic ensures that if you need to rerun HARMONY — for example to
evaluate different drop fractions, add new samples, or adjust bamCoverage
parameters — the expensive BAM preparation step is not repeated unnecessarily.

## Reuse and Resume

HARMONY is designed to support iterative analysis workflows where parameters are
adjusted between runs without repeating expensive computation.

The most common scenario is evaluating different drop fractions while keeping
the same samples and BAR file — for example, after running CODA and deciding to
explore a more conservative threshold. In this case, set
`reuse_baseline_from_run` to the absolute path of a prior run directory. HARMONY
will copy or symlink the baseline bigWigs from that run, skip BAM preparation
and baseline bigWig generation entirely, and proceed directly to scale factor
computation and scaled bigWig generation with the new parameters. This is also
useful when adding new samples to an existing experiment or when re-running with
adjusted bamCoverage settings.

If `copy_reused_baseline_bw` is true (default), baseline bigWigs are physically
copied into the new run directory, making the new run fully self-contained. If
false, symlinks are created instead, which is faster but means the new run
depends on the prior run directory remaining in place.

## Choosing Drop Fractions

The drop fraction controls how aggressively HARMONY trims high-signal BARs
before computing the background median. Higher drop fractions remove more BARs
and produce scale factors based on lower-signal regions; lower drop fractions
retain more BARs and may include some with real antibody signal.

HARMONY computes scale factors at all configured drop fractions in a single run,
producing a separate set of scaled bigWigs for each. This allows users to
evaluate the effect of different drop fractions without rerunning the pipeline.

The recommended drop fractions `[0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50]` span a practical range
for most CUT&RUN and CUT&TAG experiments. For most marks, drop0.20 is a
reasonable starting point. CODA (Pipeline 3) evaluates all drop fractions and
can help identify which produces the most consistent normalization for a given
experiment. In particular, CODA's `secondary_outputs/drop_stability_by_experiment.tsv`
and `drop_recommendation.tsv` rank drops by the cross-replicate reproducibility
of the depth-normalized (CPM) background estimate (medBAR), which is a 
non-circular signal of drop quality; treat the suggestion as advisory and 
confirm it against a positive control before locking quantitative claims.

A sample will drop out at a given threshold — meaning no valid scale factor can
be computed and no scaled bigWig is produced — when coverage over BAR regions is
too low. At aggressive drop fractions, more BARs are trimmed from the top, and
if the remaining values are all zero or too sparse to compute a reliable median,
the scale factor is recorded as NA. This typically reflects insufficient
sequencing coverage over anchor regions for that sample, not unusually high
signal. Dropout at conservative thresholds (low drop fraction such as drop0.10)
is a more serious warning sign and is flagged by CODA.

## Notes on Coverage Geometry and MAPQ

**Canonical bin size is fixed at 1 bp.** Larger bins are available only for
optional downstream derivatives.

**`min_mapq: 0` is the default, not an invariant.** MAPQ 0 matches discovery of
the distributed dm6 panel. A higher threshold can reduce BAR dynamic range and
may change the ordering of weak BARs, but it does not change BAR coordinates.
HARMONY therefore permits higher MAPQ values while requiring the same threshold
for canonical NONE, CPM, and scaled tracks within a run. The threshold is
recorded in `coverage_contract.tsv`.

**Centering and extension are separate user-facing output choices, but not
independent deepTools switches.** Centering is excluded from BAR measurement but
may be applied to downstream derivatives after the BASIN scalar is determined.
For paired-end centered/read-length derivatives, HARMONY passes deepTools both
`--centerReads` and `--extendReads`; deepTools uses the inferred fragment to find
the center and then emits a read-length footprint there. With `--extendReads`
alone, coverage spans the inferred fragment, making integrated coverage depend on
fragment length; this is prohibited on the canonical path. Note that centered
output is not guaranteed to preserve base-by-base integrated coverage *exactly*
for reads with clipping/indels, because deepTools centers using inferred query
length whereas canonical coverage follows aligned reference blocks. That does not
alter the BASIN scalar itself because centered tracks are downstream derivatives
only.
